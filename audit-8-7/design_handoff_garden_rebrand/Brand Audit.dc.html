02 — Keep this

Four things /about gets right

Whoever wrote these pages knew what they were doing. Any consolidation should move toward them, not away.

Three doors

Creatives, patrons, leaders — split at the top

A two-sided market with a third gatekeeper audience is hard to pitch in one paragraph. Splitting the route immediately is the right call, and each page stays in its reader's language.

Real prices

$10 / $25 / $50 and $500+, on the page

Faith-adjacent platforms usually hide money behind “learn more.” Naming the number is a trust move and a qualifier — it does the filtering the sales conversation would.

One named human

Shua, in the San Diego music scene

A specific person, a specific city, a specific funder. It is the single most convincing artifact across all four pages, and it appears exactly once. It should be a pattern, not a garnish.

The 50% line

“Half of all dues fund other projects”

This is the mechanism that makes it an economy rather than a subscription. It is buried mid-page on one of three routes. It belongs on the homepage, above the fold.

03 — The problems

Five, in the order they cost you money

01

The front door is blank

thecrossboard.org is a client-rendered shell. To Google, to a link preview in a group text, to an LLM answering “what is TheCrossBoard,” the homepage is the word “Loading…”. The /about pages are static and fully tagged — so the sub-pages outrank and out-share the brand's own front door.

02

Two names, three domains, one product

A patron arrives at TheCrossBoard, reads a page branded The Garden, and clicks a button to thegarden.app. Three identities in under a minute. In a category built on trust — money moving between churches, donors, and artists — an identity that shifts under the visitor's feet is the most expensive kind of friction.

The path today
thecrossboard.org  →  “TheCrossBoard”
/about  →  “The Garden”
CTA  →  thegarden.app
footer  →  “← Back to TheCrossBoard”
03

You never say the word “marketplace”

Across four pages the product is community, patronage, resources, tables, fellowship, storytelling. It is never an exchange. If this is a marketplace for the Christian creative economy, the pages describe the feeling of it and never the mechanism — so a patron can't tell what they get, and a creative can't tell what they're listing.

Words used instead
communitypatronagefellowshiptablesseatscultivateflourishingback them
04

Six metaphors, none compounding

Cross. Board. Garden. Table. Fellowship. Guide — who is also a pathfinder, a director, or a coach. Each is warm on its own; stacked, they stop building a picture and start reading as vocabulary. A member has to learn four nouns before they understand what tier they're on.

Tier names in play
Host a project · Host up to five · Guide · Pathfinder · Director · Coach · Fellowship · Project · Seats at the Table · Your whole church
05

The tagline drifts on the money page

Two of three pages close with “A home for Kingdom-minded creatives.” The patrons page closes with “For the flourishing of the arts.” That's the one page in front of people writing five-figure checks, and it's the one page that drops the faith frame. Either the frame is the pitch or it isn't — split it and you weaken both readings.

Footer lines
creatives → A home for Kingdom-minded creatives.
leaders → A home for Kingdom-minded creatives.
patrons → For the flourishing of the arts.
04 — Recommendations

What I'd do, in order

The one decision everything else waits on

Lead with The Garden. Retire CrossBoard to the letterhead.

Every piece of finished brand work you have is already Garden work — the voice, the metaphor, the tier names, the app domain the buttons point at. CrossBoard carries a board-and-listings metaphor from an earlier version of the product, sits in a crowded namespace of unrelated “Crossboard” businesses, and appears on exactly one surface: the blank one. Keeping both means paying twice for recognition you'd get once.

The counter-case, honestly: “Cross” is explicit where “Garden” is coded — a church board reading a one-line description will parse CrossBoard's affiliation instantly and may not parse The Garden's at all. If the buyer is institutional and cold, that legibility is worth real money. The answer there isn't to keep two brands; it's to make The Garden's descriptor carry the explicitness the name doesn't.

R1

Server-render the homepage this week

Cheapest, highest-return fix on the list, and it needs no naming decision. Ship a static homepage with a real title, description, and OG image. You already have the template that works: /about is the proof.

EffortDays. No design work required.
R2

Collapse to one domain, 301 the other

Pick thegarden.app as the canonical home and permanently redirect thecrossboard.org to it, marketing pages included. One address on every card, deck, and Instagram bio. Hold the old domain — never link to it.

Watch forAny printed or granted material citing the old URL.
R3

Write the descriptor that does the explaining

A soft name needs a hard descriptor sitting next to it, every time, in the same lockup. This is where “marketplace,” “Christian,” and “creative economy” earn their place — not in the name, in the line beneath it.

CandidatesThree drafted below.
R4

Cut to two metaphors: the Garden and the Table

The Garden is the whole; the Table is the unit people sit at. Both already do work across all three pages. Retire Board entirely. Collapse Guide / Pathfinder / Director / Coach into one word — Guide — and let Fellowship keep its single, precise meaning: an ongoing stipend to a named artist.

TestCan a new member name their tier out loud, correctly, after one read?
R5

Put the 50% mechanism and one artist on the homepage

“Half of every membership funds someone else's work” is the sentence that turns a subscription into an economy, and Shua is the proof it runs. Both are currently three clicks deep. The homepage should carry the mechanism, one named artist, and the three doors — nothing else.

ThenMake the artist story a repeating format, not a one-off.
R6

One tagline, including in front of donors

Pick “A home for Kingdom-minded creatives” and use it on every page including patrons — or decide the faith frame is a second-screen detail and remove it consistently. The current split lets each audience suspect the other is getting the real pitch.

My readKeep it. The frame is the moat, not the liability.
05 — Drafts

Three descriptors to test

Each sits directly under the wordmark and does the work the name deliberately doesn't.

A · plainest

The marketplace for the Christian creative economy.

Says the category out loud. Best for grant applications, press, and anyone who needs to file you somewhere. Coldest of the three.

B · mechanism-first

Where Christian creatives find patrons — and half of every membership funds someone else's work.

Longer, but it's the only line that explains the flywheel. Strongest on the homepage; too long for a lockup.

C · closest to today

A home for Kingdom-minded creatives and the people who back them.

Already written, already on the page, already two-sided. Warmest — and the only one that never tells a stranger what the product does.
