Brand framework · v2 Tenancy · tables · domain · new visual direction

The Garden holds the tables. Two metaphors, and no more.

Tables turn out to be a platform primitive, not one tenant's habit. The mixed-metaphor worry deserved a real answer instead of a dodge. Support means money and hands. And the cream-and-serif palette is gone — you were right that it read like everyone else.

Revision one

Tables are yours, not one tenant's

If Abiding Practice would also run tables — discussion, spiritual formation, photography — then Table is the platform's unit of gathering. It just has to flex on size, place and term. Section 04.

Revision two

“Back” was too narrow

It reads as money only. Support is funding, collaboration and participation — nobody here works alone, and the descriptor should say so. Section 05.

Revision three

New visual direction

Rebuilt around the one artifact your product manufactures: a credit. Archive, not startup. Section 07.

01 — Today

Two identities, nothing shared

Not the mark, palette, typeface, ground or button. And the homepage still server-renders nothing but “Loading…” — no title, no description, no social preview. That fix is independent of every decision below and should ship regardless of what you name things.

TheCrossBoard — gradient type, gradient CTA, 2×2 tech mark. Reads as a dark-mode social network; nothing says patronage.
The Garden — white paper, forest green, old-style serif. Credible, but hard to read: 15px grey on white, prices in a far-right rail.
02 — Architecture

Three tiers, and only one is yours to name

Two brands on your site was never the real problem — a platform with tenants needs more than one. The problem was that both of yours were competing to be the top one.

Tier 1 · yours

The Garden

The platform. Owns the domain, sign-in, the money rails, the public story pages, and the Table primitive. One name, one visual system.

Tier 2 · theirs

Host organizations

Abiding Practice. Table Art Society. Churches. Their own name and voice, their own tables, credited on every project their money touches.

Tier 3 · shared

Tables

One primitive, configured per host. Table Art Society's run ten people for nine months; Abiding Practice's might be virtual and open. Same object, different settings.

The rule that falls out of this

Words that touch money are yours and fixed — Project, Fellowship, Seat, Patron, Creative mean the same thing in every host's space, or your accounting and your story pages break. Everything about how a particular table runs is the host's to set. That's your whole vocabulary policy in one line.

03 — The metaphor budget

Two. Garden and Table. That's the allowance.

Why it isn't mixed

A table in a garden is a scene people can picture — a long table under trees, a harvest meal. Metaphors only clash when they can't share a frame. Garden gives you the place; Table gives you the gathering. Different jobs, one picture.

What actually kills it

Not the second metaphor — the third. Today you also run board, fellowship, pathfinder, seed, journey. Six images is vocabulary, not a brand. Cap it at two and enforce it in copy review.

Banned from here on

Seeds, soil, roots, watering, harvest as verbs. Pathways, journeys, bridges, ecosystems, unlocking. “Water the garden” as a donate button. The garden is a place you are, not a chore you do.

One overlap, and it's a feature: a seat is both a place at a capped table and the thing a patron buys for someone. Same idea from two directions — a patron buys a creative a seat. Keep one word and let it carry both.

04 — The Table primitive

One object, four settings

The trap is shipping four types — Virtual Table, Open Table, Cohort Table, Church Table — and ending up with a matrix nobody can explain. Ship one Table with settings. A host answers four questions and the table configures itself.

05 — Descriptor

Support, not backing

You're right that “back” collapses to money. Support is money, hands and time — and “we don't work alone” is the sharpest thing you've said in this whole conversation. It should be in the identity, not just in the philosophy.

The descriptor — use everywhere, beside the name

Where Kingdom-minded creatives find the people who support their work.

The line under it — carries the collaboration half

Nobody makes anything alone.

Four words, and it does three jobs: it says support isn't only financial, it makes a patron's non-cash contribution legitimate, and it's the one sentence a creative reads and feels rather than evaluates. It also happens to be true of your 50% mechanism — everyone here is funding someone.

Support means, concretely

Funding a project or a Fellowship · covering a seat · collaborating on the work · mentoring · showing up to the thing.

So the patron CTA changes

Not “Water the garden.” Try “Support a creative” — and let the next screen offer money, a seat, or your time as equal options. Right now only money is a door.

And the retired word

Drop back / backed by from headlines. Keep it only where you literally mean cash, e.g. “this project is 60% funded.”

06 — The domain

Don't buy thegarden.app yet

Not because $128 is a lot — because the order is wrong. Buying the exact-match domain is the last step of a naming decision, not the first, and you have one unresolved dependency in front of it.

The dependency

Trademark screen first

“The Garden” is a crowded mark. If it's unworkable in classes 42 and 36, a domain you're renewing forever is money spent on a name you can't defend. The screen takes days and costs less than two years of the domain.

The price is a signal

$128/yr is premium pricing

A standard .app runs roughly $15–20/yr. At $128 you're on a premium tier that renews at that rate forever — about $1,300 a decade, and you never own it outright. Worth it for a name you've committed to; not for one you're still testing.

And the TLD fights you

.app says consumer app

You're selling to churches and arts organizations and taking patronage money. .org matches what you actually are, matches the domain you already have, and costs about a tenth as much. Check thegarden.org and a two-word .org before you settle.

What to do this week instead

Point creatives.exchange — which you already own, at no additional cost — at the site today. It reads as a phrase rather than a label, it says “creatives” to a stranger in a way “thegarden.app” never will, and “exchange” here means the mutual kind: money, collaboration, time. It's a better campaign and card URL than either domain you're weighing, and it buys you the weeks you need to clear the name.

Then: if the trademark clears and you commit to The Garden, buy the exact-match domain that week and make it canonical — at that point $128/yr is trivially worth owning your own name, and I'd stop debating it. Keep thecrossboard.org registered forever and 301 it. Never let two live domains serve the same content.

07 — Visual direction

Built on the credit, not the mood

In creative work the currency is credit — film titles, liner notes, a gallery wall label, “commissioned by.” Your product literally manufactures credits: “sponsored by a neighborhood church.” So build the system from that artifact: monospace metadata, a big tight grotesque for names, hairline rules, one acid accent. It reads as an archive of work rather than a startup — right for designers and filmmakers, and structurally hostile to slop. No gradients, no stock photography, no pastel rounded cards, no serif.

Why a grotesque, not a serif
Bricolage Grotesque is variable, slightly irregular, and unmistakably contemporary — it looks drawn rather than licensed. A book serif signals heritage and taste; you need to signal now, because your users are the people who judge that.
Why monospace metadata
It's the language of credits, call sheets, and archives — factual, unglamorous, trustworthy. It makes “25 seats · covered by Grace Chapel” read as a record rather than marketing, which is exactly the credibility patronage needs.
The accent
One acid citron, used as a mark and a highlight — never a fill, never a gradient. It's the growth note the garden earns without a single leaf, and it's the opposite of the blurple every faith-tech product ships with.
Imagery rule
Every image on the site is work made by someone on it, credited in mono underneath. No stock, no illustration, no drawn sprout. The constraint is also the proof — and it means the site gets better as the platform does.

Order of operations

Cheapest and most reversible first

This week

Server-render the homepage — real title, description, OG image. Point creatives.exchange at it. Order the trademark screen. No naming decision required for any of the three.

Next

Lock the six words and the descriptor. Find-and-replace across the product, the flyers and the invite emails. Vocabulary is far cheaper to change than design.

Buy the exact-match domain and make it canonical. 301 thecrossboard.org and keep it registered forever. One live domain, always.

Then the pixels

Wordmark, type, one accent, no gradients — shipped to marketing and the app in the same release. Don't roll a new brand out to one surface only; that's how you got here.
