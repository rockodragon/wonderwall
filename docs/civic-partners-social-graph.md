## 0 · Why this document exists

On Sept 15 Rick met **Jonathon Glus** — the Conrad Prebys Foundation's Senior Fellow for Art & Design in residence at the Downtown San Diego Partnership, executive director of the new downtown **Art + Design District**, board president of California for the Arts, and (Nov 2018–Oct 2025) executive director of the City of San Diego's Commission for Arts and Culture. He knows Haley. He said San Diego has more going on right now than San Francisco or Los Angeles and could serve as a model. §11 tests that claim; the short version is that it holds on momentum and money-per-artist and does not hold on raw scale.

Glus's own agenda, in his words (San Diego Magazine, Sept 14, 2026; Prebys "Stop & Talk," Apr 30, 2026): a downtown district "for creatives, creative education, and the creative workforce — not tourists"; connect the anchor institutions (Symphony, Opera, Broadway San Diego, MCASD, Balboa Park) to working artists who live and work in the neighborhood; affordability and space for creative workers; and build the "contemporary art product" San Diego never intentionally invested in (fairs, biennials, residencies), benchmarked against Chicago, London, Miami, Dubai. "If you don't have an artist at your dinner table, it's not a dinner party." That thesis is our thesis with a civic accent, which is why this map starts with his orbit and works outward.

The second thread is **Haley → Prebys**. On June 6 Haley emailed Rick asking him to nominate her for the 2027 Prebys Leadership Awards ($100k, individual leaders) and wrote that she has "a couple of relationships I have been cultivating over there." Nominations closed July 23; the selection committee meets in October, recipients are told late Nov–early Dec, and the public announcement is February 2027. Nothing about her standing is public yet and nothing here should be repeated as if it were.

**How to read this.** Every organization is scored on what it can bring — **money, space, people, doors** — and on the warm path we already have. Filings are the latest 990 on ProPublica. Grant windows are in §9 sorted by next deadline, with a column for *who applies* (the platform company is for-profit; TAS is a 501(c)(3) since July 2024; Abiding Practice is a nonprofit; anything else needs a fiscal sponsor). ⚠ marks a figure from a secondary or stale source; §13 lists them all. The same content is in three CSVs under `docs/data/` for the UpSight import.

## 11 · Is Glus right? San Diego vs San Francisco vs Los Angeles

**Verdict in one line:** the claim holds on *direction and coordination* and fails on *scale and trajectory*. Say "San Diego is moving faster and is better coordinated than either city right now." Do not say "bigger" or "better funded." The line Rick heard is a private remark; no public source for it was found, so don't attribute it to Glus on stage unless he says it there.

### Where it holds
- **The June 2026 coalition.** Mayor Gloria's FY27 draft proposed cutting the Commission for Arts and Culture's hotel-tax grants ~85%, from $13.8M to about $2M. Within weeks, Council President Pro Tem Kent Lee, Budget Committee chair Henry Foster III, County Supervisor Monica Montgomery Steppe and the Prebys Foundation announced a **$10.35M restoration**: $6M redirected from Convention Center hotel-tax funds, **$3M direct from Prebys**, $1.3M in restored competitive grants.
