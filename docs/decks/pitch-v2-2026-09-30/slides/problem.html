The problem, in one breath: working creatives here are isolated and underpaid, and the organizations that support them run on three or four rented tools taped together. Arts funding is tight, and San Diego creatives leave or stop making work. Left stack, creatives: isolated (no table, no peers, nobody asking), underpaid (three jobs, and the creative work is the one that does not pay), posting into the void, and not worthy, the story they start to believe. Right stack, businesses and organizations: rented tools taped together, no trusted way to find and pay local creatives, empty rooms, and sponsors who cannot see where the money went. Each pain on the left has a counterpart on the right, and the platform is the bridge. Keep it about people and local businesses, not about grant budgets. In the age of AI the gap gets wider: creative output is easy to generate, so the question of who gets paid for making it, and how businesses find and pay real people, becomes more urgent.
