This slide is about people, not institutions. Read the ring left column first, then right. Three jobs: the creative work is the one that does not pay, so it gets the leftover hours. Posting into the void: Instagram, hoping to be noticed. Alone: no table, no peers, nobody asking how the work is going. Not worthy: the story they start to believe. The emotional cost and the financial cost are the same cost. Isolation drains confidence, and with it the invitations, the referrals, the paid work and the projects that never get started. Then the bar: the economy loses their gifts. Nothing of theirs shows up in the places of work, restaurants, bars, parks and cultural districts, which is where creatives grow business activity when they take part. Numbers to answer questions with: San Diego County has roughly 26,000 working creatives (range 22,000 to 30,000); the city's FY2027 arts budget is $10.35M after a proposed 85% cut was mostly restored, and almost none of it reaches an individual creative. Do not turn this into a talk about institutions receiving money.
