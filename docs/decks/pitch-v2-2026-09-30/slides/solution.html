The "after" of the problem slide, same layout: the two stacks become what creatives and organizations get, and the exchange is the bridge in the middle with the services on it: gatherings and events, memberships and seats, paid work, project backings, classes, fellowship programs. Read the stacks against the problem slide: isolated becomes belonging (a community that knows your name, in person and online); underpaid becomes paid (paid work with the pay stated up front, and creatives keep 90% of every backing); posting into the void becomes seen (a portfolio, an audience, work in the city); not worthy becomes worthy (fellowships that buy time, peers who ask). Tools taped together becomes one platform; can't find local talent becomes vetted local talent; empty rooms become full rooms; sponsorship with no return becomes outcomes in the six-month impact report. The bar at the bottom names the three outcomes the next slide measures: financial, social and emotional, cultural. Communities are run by their own people; people join a community, not a platform. What is live today: profiles, projects, paid work listings, events with public RSVP, communities and classes, covered seats, program pages, and backing checkout on Stripe. Membership is $10 a month and half of it funds the fellowship program, run by our 501(c)(3) partner. Soft launch was September 28, 2026; two communities are live with The Table Art Society, The Grove, Abiding Practice and Temple.
