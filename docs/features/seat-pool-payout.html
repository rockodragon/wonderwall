Seat, Pool, Payout

Where the money gates, where it flows, and where it stops. Built from the code as it runs today, the plan docs, the beads tracker, a competitive pass on eleven platforms, and two independent pressure-tests (UX and PM). Meant to be argued with.

2026-09-15 · code-verified against app/convex/garden · live plan: creatives-exchange-discussion-brief.md v0.5 (2026-09-08) · the code implements the Aug 31 model · every delta is marked
Three canons, one code base.The plan (Sept 8), the build spec (Aug 31) and the product plan (Aug 9) disagree on whose the seat is, how dues split, and who pays card fees. The code built the middle one. That's the circle.
Dues are the engine, not the revenue.Under the plan a $10 seat leaves the platform $1 — a real $1, processing paid by the payer. Under the code, ~$4.41. Either way the margin is in the 10% funding lanes.
Two of three seat gates aren't enforced.Starting a project and applying to paid work are free today. The code that would gate them exists, tested, and is not called.
Money in works. Money out barely exists.Backings have no payout tracking at all. Host earnings are a manual ledger. Stripe Connect: specified for Phase 3, no bead until today, no code.

3 · The money map — in, split, out

As built. Every split is computed on the gross, and the platform's share absorbs Stripe's fee. The plan differs on two counts: dues split 10% platform / 90% the community's to divide (The Garden: 40% runs the group · 50% its pool · 10% platform), and card processing is added at checkout and paid by the payer — "the platform's 10% is a real 10%," because the financial model shows small monthly charges would otherwise eat most of the platform's share. The pledge and class lanes match the plan. Then look at the right column: money in is live on all three lanes; money out is where the model stops.

  • Zero free projects is an outlier. Every comparable lets you create and publish free and gates monetization.
  • Gating "apply" outright mirrors Upwork Connects — the most criticized mechanic in the set, and the one that punishes exactly who the pool is for.
  • "Apply free, seat to be accepted" has precedent in Dribbble's ranking boosts and Fiverr's Top-Rated favoring — discovery open, monetization at the success moment.
  • No one else ties the paywall to a redistribution pool. The $10 is doing double duty. Frame it the way Kickstarter frames its 5% — shared infrastructure — not as feature restriction.

5 · Pressure test — where UX, PM and the field agree

Two independent passes, no shared notes. Where they split, both positions are shown; the recommendation is mine.

Should a free account be able to start a project?

UX analyst"Draft free, publish with a seat." A profile isn't a taste; a project is. Converts at maximum sunk cost, keeps never-paying drafts out of the feed.
PMKeep 0 as the standing rule — it's the second-clearest paywall moment. Time-box a free-creation exception through the Nov 6 launch to solve cold-start liquidity.
Field0 free is an outlier. Behance, Beehiiv, Dribbble, Contra all let you build and publish free.
The plan (Sept 8)"Asking for support requires you to pay or join first." Starting a project isn't named; seeking backing is. Draft-free fits that line as written.
Recommend: draft free, publish with a seat — as the permanent rule. It answers the liquidity worry (the work exists, the lead sees it), keeps the gate, and puts the ask at the moment someone has already invested. Add the launch-window exception on top only if the feed still looks empty in October.

Where does paid work gate — apply, accept, or payout?

UX analystAccept-time is the better moment — highest intent in the whole funnel. Kill the sort-by-tier idea: it's pay-to-win and a silent gate nobody can see or act on.
PMGate at accept now; it grows the exact scarce thing (visible paid-project activity). Migrate to a % of payout once Connect ships — they aren't mutually exclusive.
FieldDribbble reversed a discovery paywall for throttling lead flow. Upwork's pay-to-apply is the sector's most-hated fee.
The plan (Sept 8)"Job postings are public. Applying takes membership. Seeing the work is the reason to join." — gate at apply, as written.
Recommend: move the plan's line one step later: anyone applies; rank by fit, never by payment status; membership is required to be accepted onto a paid job. Copy at that moment: "You got the job — join The Garden to accept it. $10 a month, half of it to the community's project pool." Revisit as a % of payout once Connect exists. The plan's version is defensible; the evidence leans this way. Your call, and it changes one sentence in the brief.

Does Five ($25) deserve to exist?

UX analystA volume tier wearing a value tier's clothes. Cut it; sell extra slots as a $5/mo add-on. Free → Seat → Host is a cleaner ladder.
PMSame diagnosis, don't cut blind: instrument it. If under 5% of seats hit the 1-project cap within 60 days, collapse it.
FieldNobody prices on a concurrent-project count. Capacity tiers work when paired with a perk (Dribbble's brief credits), not alone.
The plan (Sept 8)No Five, no $50 tier. Free member / Member at the community's price / Host at $0.
Recommend: follow the plan — one membership per community, no rungs. If a project cap is wanted, make it a Seat add-on ("+ more projects, $5/mo each") rather than a tier. If the code's 1/5/10 ladder is kept anyway, measure cap-hits for 60 days before selling it.

Host $50 vs. "hosting is free"

UX analystA naming collision that's actively confusing today — the pricing page already needs a footnote every time the word appears.
PMTwo docs describe two products under one name. Aug 31 is canon: free + 10% on sales + $50 for programs. And "$50 to run a grant program" is vaporware before Connect — a pilot pitch to AP/TAS, not a self-serve line.
FieldSkool, Circle and Mighty charge the owner a SaaS fee for tools and take-rate. Charging hosts nothing and taking 10% is the cleaner story; keep it clean.
The plan (Sept 8)"Hosting is never rented" — $0, 90% of what classes collect. There is no $50 tier in the plan at all.
Recommend: follow the plan: no $50 tier on the pricing page, and "Host" means the person who runs a class. If community grant programs ever need a SKU, name it "Leader" and hand-sell it until Connect exists. Retire the $50 level from the code's capability matrix when the reconcile pass happens.

Who pays card processing?

The plan (Sept 8)The payer, added at checkout (~3% + 30¢). "The platform's 10% is a real 10%." The financial model (Inputs!B9) shows the platform can't run on 10% minus card fees on small monthly charges.
The code (Aug 31)The opposite: the platform absorbs Stripe out of its own share on every lane. Nets ~7% on a $100 pledge, ~4% on $10. The receipt copy "$5 funds… $5 runs the place" assumes this.
FieldMost pass processing to the creator (Kickstarter, Substack, Patreon, Fiverr). Payer-covers at checkout is the ticketing norm (Eventbrite-style) and common as a "cover the fee" line.
Recommend: the plan stands — it's the version with a financial model behind it. That means the code's duesSplit and the split copy on receipts and the /for pages have to move to it in the reconcile pass; until then the site promises a split the plan has already rejected.

7 · Decisions on the table

Each with a recommended default. Change the default, not the list.

  1. Which canon. The plan (per-community seat · 10% platform, 90% the community's · payer covers processing · no tiers) or the code (platform-wide seat · 50/50 · platform absorbs · 1/5/10). The README already answers this: the plan. Then the code, the receipts and the /for pages move — that's a scoped reconcile pass, not a copy tweak: memberships gain a required community, duesSplit changes, the pricing page loses two tiers.
  2. Free project. Draft free, publish with a seat. Fits the plan's "asking for support requires you to join first." Alternative: keep 0 with a launch-window exception.
  3. Paid work. Apply free, rank by fit, membership to be accepted. No tier sorting. Revises one sentence in the brief ("applying takes membership" → "being hired takes membership"). Wire the gate into decideRequest, not requestToJoin.
  4. Tiers. Follow the plan: none. If a project cap is wanted, a Seat add-on, not Five. If 1/5/10 is kept regardless, measure cap-hits for 60 days first.
  5. The $50 tier. Not in the plan. Off the pricing page; grant programs hand-sold until Connect. If it ever returns as a SKU, call it Leader — "Host" is the person who runs a class.
  6. Events. Free for informal gatherings; keep the membership gate for private/ticketed events. No create-event mutation exists today, so this costs nothing to decide.
  7. Payout rail. Bead wonderwall-7avu: owed-to-creative ledger row before Nov 6; Connect Express in Phase 3. Until then the hosts page's "you keep 90 cents" is an owed balance paid by hand — say so.
  8. Card processing. The plan's answer stands: the payer, at checkout. Code and receipt copy currently say the opposite.
