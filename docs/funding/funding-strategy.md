# Funding strategy: the sober version

2026-09-30. This is the funding document. The [CFO memo](cfo-memo-ask-and-spend.md) covers operations (entity, pay, team, spend); [Where the money is](where-the-money-is.md) holds the research and the sources. This one says who will fund us now, who will not until we meet a stated requirement, and who will not at all.

## The situation in four sentences

The company is a two-week-old soft launch with no revenue, and even the franchise plan (20 metros, $2.1M of revenue and $672k of EBITDA in year five) is a 3x to 6x outcome for a pre-seed investor, not a venture one. Almost every institution that funds early companies in San Diego funds biotech and software for a chance at 10x, and every creative-economy or impact investor we found has a revenue floor we will not clear for one to three years. Foundations fund nonprofits, not us. The money for the next twelve months is customers, the people who already love these communities, small investors through a community round, and a zero-interest microloan.

## Prebys, plainly

1. **Prebys will not invest in the company in the next two years.** Its venture fund is tech and healthcare. Its creative-economy investing runs through Upstart's criteria: seed or Series A, $500k of trailing revenue, a committed lead. It publishes no PRI sizes and its grants are invitation-based. The survey exists so they can decide what to build; being counted is worth twenty minutes and nothing more.
2. **Prebys grants go to nonprofits, for the nonprofit's own program.** Its published arts grantees are secular arts organizations. Abiding Practice is a church-affiliated nonprofit, and foundations as a rule do not fund religious activity; assume a Prebys grant to Abiding Practice is not available unless the program is structured and described as an open, secular arts program and Prebys's eligibility language is checked first. The Table Art Society, if it is a secular arts nonprofit with its own board, is the plausible grantee, and the grant would fund its cohort program, its stipends and its events.
3. **A grant to a partner does not fund the platform.** The platform is one vendor to that program at list price: seat packs and an administration fee. If Haley or Rick sits on both sides of that purchase, it is a related-party transaction: it needs approval by the partner's disinterested board, fair-market pricing that anyone else pays, and disclosure to the funder, and a program officer will look for exactly that. Expected value to the company from a Prebys-funded partner program in year one: $0 of investment and perhaps $5k to $15k of fees and seats. It is not in the budget.
4. **What Prebys is good for now:** the relationship (the survey, the inquiry form, a conversation with the program officer about the creative-economy work), a sponsorship of November 6 hosted by a partner, and being on the list when they build the vehicle in 2027 or 2028. Ask for that, and nothing else.
