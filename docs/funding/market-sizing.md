# Market sizing: how many creatives would take part

Prepared 2026-09-30 for the fresh look at the theCreative.exchange financial model. Every figure is cited. ⚠ marks a figure that is secondary, derived, or not yet checked at the source page. "Est." rows are our arithmetic, not published counts. The companion workbook is `docs/financial-model-5yr-v2.xlsx` (Market sheet).

**Short answer.** San Diego County has roughly 22,000 to 30,000 people whose primary occupation is creative (central estimate 26,000), and a wider pool of 45,000 or more with any paid creative activity. Adding church creative volunteers and the mapped Christian creative networks gives a serviceable market of about 26,000 to 45,000 people (central 35,000). At benchmark conversion rates for paid communities, that supports roughly 500 to 1,750 paying individual members in three years, central about 1,000. That is the honest ceiling for a San Diego-only, individual-seat business. The larger numbers in the model come from three things the old model did not count: organizations buying seats in packs, hosts and partners paying for the platform, and a second and third metro reached through partner networks.

**Working creatives in San Diego County (people whose primary occupation is creative).** Three ways to get there:

- **Method A, NEA share × labor force.** The NEA's 2015-19 artist share was 1.457% nationally and 2.234% in California. Applied to the county labor force: about 24,300 at the national share, 37,300 at the California share, and about 26,200 when California is scaled by San Diego's lower location quotient (0.99 vs 1.41).
- **Method B, OEWS grossed up for self-employment.** The NEA reports 33.6% of artists are self-employed. Dividing the wage-and-salary counts by 0.664 gives 21,600 (from 14,370) to 30,100 (from 19,990).
- **Method C, outer bound.** The 82,463 creative-economy jobs (43,968 self-employed) is a job count that includes side income, not a headcount.

**Range: 22,000 to 30,000 people; central 26,000.** The broader "any paid creative activity" pool is plausibly 45,000 or more (inferred from the self-employed job count, ⚠ not a headcount).

Not found: an Artists Count participant count, an NEA metro table for San Diego, an EDC self-employed creative count.

## 6. Sizing ladder

**TAM, United States.** 3.8M paid creatives (NEA 2024) plus 0.53M to 1.37M church creative volunteers ⚠, about **4.3M to 5.2M people** before overlap. At $120 a year per seat that is **$0.52B to $0.62B a year** in seat revenue alone, before backings, classes, events and organization plans.

**SAM, San Diego County plus reachable Christian networks, three years.**

| Segment | Low | Mid | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| SD working creatives | 22,000 | 26,000 | 30,000 |
| SD church creative volunteers ⚠ | 2,200 | 4,000 | 5,700 |
| Christian network members ⚠ | 4,600 | 9,200 | 13,800 |
| Sum before overlap | 28,800 | 39,200 | 49,500 |
| **SAM after 10% overlap haircut** | **26,000** | **35,000** | **45,000** |

**Paying individual members at benchmark conversion**

| SAM | 1% | 3% | 5% |
|---|---|---|---|
| 26,000 | 260 | 780 | 1,300 |
| 35,000 | 350 | 1,050 | 1,750 |
| 45,000 | 450 | 1,350 | 2,250 |

**SOM, three-year paying individual members: about 1,000 (range 500 to 1,750).** About 3% of the mid SAM, or roughly $120K a year in seat revenue at $10 a month. Hope*Writers' 3,200 paying members ⚠ is the only documented comparable, and it took years and a national audience.

**SOM including schools, universities, churches and other organizations: 5,000 to 10,000 members in three years (founder estimate, 2026-09-30, not yet sourced).** This is the individual figure above (about 1,000) plus seats bought by organizations for their people. It is consistent with the model: the Base case reaches 5,700 paying and covered members in year three, Conservative 1,270 and Fundable 12,700. To source it, count San Diego universities and colleges, school districts, churches and other organizations, and multiply by a realistic seat-pack size (ten organizations at thirty seats is 300 members).

**What the ladder leaves out, and why the model is bigger than the SOM.** Individual conversion is the wrong ceiling for this business. The model's Base case reaches its member counts through channels the ladder cannot benchmark: organizations buying seats in packs (churches, universities, districts; for example ten churches at thirty seats is 300 covered members from ten sales calls), hosts and partner communities paying a platform plan, 10% of backings and class sales, event fees, and program-administration fees. It also assumes a second and third metro by years three and four through the mapped partner networks. See `docs/funding/model-fresh-look.md`.

## Verify before quoting

1. Re-check the BLS metro figures at [data.bls.gov/oes](https://data.bls.gov/oes/) (area 41740); the sub-group sums fall short of 19,990 by 510 because of suppressed rows.
2. The 22,000 to 30,000 range rests on NEA 2015-19 shares (pre-COVID; "artist" includes architects). The 43,968 self-employed figure is a job count including side income.
3. All church-volunteer counts are assumptions; no source counts worship-team members.
4. Patreon and Kickstarter figures are secondary because both sites blocked automated reads.
5. Hope*Writers price and member count need the source page; ACFW, Word Weavers and Realm Makers sizes are from snippets.
6. Artists Count's 42 artists, and the Comic-Con, ArtWalk, Design Week, Rock and Awaken figures are from snippets or dated sources.
7. FY2027 city arts figures differ between sources ($13.8M vs $11.8M base); check the adopted budget.
8. ARDA 2020 undercounts some congregations; Hartford's 373,000 is an estimate between two counts.
