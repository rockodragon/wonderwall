# The financial model, a fresh look

Prepared 2026-09-30. Companion files: `docs/financial-model-5yr-v2.xlsx` (the model), `docs/funding/market-sizing.md` (how many creatives are out there), `docs/financial-model-3yr.xlsx` (the Aug 28 model this replaces).

## The question

"It looks like we're all going to be poor and this doesn't look like winning. Are we being too conservative? What would make this fundable? Is it even realistic? How many creatives would take part? Model the Garden and the Exchange separately, and the revenue sources together."

## The short answer

The old model was not too conservative. It was too narrow. It had one engine: ten percent of small flows plus $10 seats, in San Diego only, sold one person at a time. That is a $50k to $150k a year business at best, and it never pays anyone. The member counts in it were, if anything, optimistic: the research says San Diego supports about 1,000 paying individual members in three years, and the old model assumed 1,500 by year two.

The new model keeps every price and take rate the community already knows (seat $10, half to the program, 90/10 on backings and classes, hosts keep 90%) and adds the four things the old one left out:

1. **Seats sold in packs** to churches and organizations. Ten churches at thirty seats is three hundred members from ten conversations, paid in one charge.
2. **Software that is paid for**: a host or organization plan, and a partner plan for people who post paid work. This is the line Skool, Circle and Patreon live on, and it is 18 to 24 percent of Base-case revenue.
3. **A national footprint** through partner communities in the networks already mapped (Hope*Writers-sized writers' groups, Realm Makers, ACFW, Word Weavers, NCVA, the Rabbit Room orbit). San Diego is the proving ground, not the market.
4. **Fees on flows the platform already carries**: five percent on event tickets and on gifts to nonprofit-run programs.

Modeled that way, the Garden alone stays what it is: a healthy community that funds real fellowships and pays for its own engagement, but not a company. The Exchange is the company.

## How many creatives would take part

The full sizing with sources is in `market-sizing.md`. The ladder:

| Ring | People | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| US paid creatives and church creatives | 4.3M to 5.2M | NEA 2024 (3.8M artists and cultural workers) plus an estimate of worship, media and design volunteers in 373,000 congregations |
| San Diego County working creatives | 22,000 to 30,000, central 26,000 | BLS May 2025 (19,990 payroll jobs in arts, design, entertainment and media) grossed up for the self-employed; the "any paid creative work" pool is 45,000 or more |
| Serviceable now: San Diego creatives, San Diego church creatives, mapped networks | 26,000 to 45,000, central 35,000 | 26,000 plus about 4,000 church creatives plus about 9,200 network members, less overlap |
| Paying individuals in three years at benchmark conversion | 500 to 1,750, central 1,000 | 1 to 5 percent free-to-paid; the median paid newsletter converts 0.6 percent, the top quartile 2 to 5 percent |

So the honest ceiling for a San Diego-only, individual-seat business is about a thousand paying people in three years, or roughly $120k a year in seats. That is the Conservative case, and it is the case that happens by default if nothing else is built.

## Revenue by source, Base case, year five

| Source | Y5 revenue | Share | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Membership dues kept (Garden house share plus 10% of partner dues and org seats) | $361k | 25% | the Garden keeps half of its seats; partner communities keep 90% of theirs |
| Backing fees, 10% | $277k | 19% | patrons backing named creatives |
| Partner plans for those who post paid work, $79/mo | $284k | 20% | PROPOSED; the most speculative line |
| Class and premium fees, 10% | $242k | 17% | hosts keep 90% |
| Sponsorships | $180k | 13% | gatherings and program sponsors, company revenue |
| Host and organization plans, $50/mo | $30k | 2% | PROPOSED |
| Program platform fee, 5% of gifts | $30k | 2% | gifts to nonprofit-run programs |
| Event ticket fee, 5% | $20k | 1% | PROPOSED; cheaper than Eventbrite's 10 to 14 percent |

Revenue per paying or covered member is $75 to $100 a year in every scenario, and the effective take on money moved falls from 24 percent to 16 percent as partner communities grow. For comparison, Patreon's average paid membership is about $80 a year gross and it keeps 8 to 12 percent; Skool charges each community $1,188 a year plus 2.9 percent, and our Base case earns $2,500 to $10,000 a year per partner community across all lines.

## Is it realistic

- **Conservative: yes.** It matches the research ladder almost exactly (1,270 paying and covered members in year three against a sized 1,000). It is also the case where everyone stays poor. It is the default.
- **Base: yes, if three things happen.** Thirty partner communities by year three and a hundred by year five, most of them outside San Diego. Organization seat packs sold to churches and universities from year two (800 seats by year three, 4,000 by year five). The two PROPOSED plans built and priced. A hundred communities is small next to Skool or Circle, and thirty by year three is one signed a month from the mapped networks. The four thousand org seats is the bet to watch: it is forty organizations at a hundred seats.
- **Fundable: only with a team.** Seventy communities by year three needs a partnerships hire in year one and a second metro by year two. It is the case an impact investor is actually buying, and it is profitable in year three.

Nothing in any scenario assumes a price increase, a public-arts grant to the company, or a single community above 170 paying members. The largest single-community assumption (170 paying members by year five in the Fundable case) is a twentieth of Hope*Writers.

## What makes it fundable, in order

1. **Sell seats in packs before selling them one at a time.** Ten churches at thirty seats by the end of year one. This is a sales motion, not a growth curve, and it is the fastest way to move a hundred people at once.
2. **Decide the PROPOSED lines now.** Host and organization plan ($50/mo), partner plan for gig posters ($79/mo), Pro seat ($25/mo), 5% on event tickets. Together they are a fifth of Base-case revenue and they are the lines investors recognize. None is in `claims.md` yet.
3. **Sign three partner communities outside San Diego in year one.** The civic graph and partner landscape already name them. One a quarter is the Base case; that pace is what turns a house community into an exchange.
4. **Fund the program through the nonprofit partner, as a managed program.** Foundations fund a managed fellowship program; they do not fund regranting. The platform earns 5% administering it and the program dollars grow the impact numbers.
5. **Count the impact every quarter.** The metrics below. A pre-revenue company with counted outcomes raises on the outcomes.
6. **Raise the full amount.** Every scenario needs about $325k before breakeven. A $150k raise buys the Conservative case with extra steps. $350k carries the Base case through year three with a cushion; total to breakeven is about $425k.

## Caveats

- The four PROPOSED lines are not in the community's claims yet; they need a decision and, for the host and partner plans, product work.
- Members are modeled as average active counts, so churn is inside the growth assumptions rather than a separate line.
- Church and organization seat counts have no public benchmark; they are the assumption to test first, in the first ten sales conversations.
- The workbook's formulas were evaluated with a Python engine (722 formulas, no errors in any scenario) because LibreOffice is unavailable in the build sandbox. Excel and Google Sheets recalculate on open. Cached values in the file are the Base case.
