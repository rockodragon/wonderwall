# creatives.exchange — outreach playbook

v0.2 · 2026-09-18 · owner: Rick

Six audiences. For each one: who they are, what they want, what to lead with, an email, a text, and what not to say.

**Money sentences come from one place.** [claims.md](claims.md) holds every sentence we say about money, word for word, and the list of things we never say. The site reads the same sentences from `app/app/constants/claims.ts`. This file names a claim in **bold** and only writes one out inside an email or a text. When a number changes, change it in claims.md first, then search this file for the old sentence.

Voice: plain and real. Headlines state the action or the fact. Numbers do the persuading. "Creative," never "artist."

---

## The story everyone hears first

> Most creative work gets found in a Facebook group and paid for out of somebody's goodwill. creatives.exchange is built to change that. It is where creatives find paid work, get backed by people who believe in them, and apply for grants. Joining is free. The Garden is the Christian creative community on it, and it is where this started. On **November 6** we put funded work on stage at Lightchurch in Encinitas: a ticketed fundraiser for the grant fund, with a free livestream.

| Audience | Lead with | Claims to use |
|---|---|---|
| Creatives | Paid work, then backing | **what it is** · **join** · **backing** · **membership** · **pool** |
| Hosts | Earning from the group they already run | **host split** |
| Patrons | One named person | **patron** |
| Churches and organizations | Covering their own creatives | **coverage** |
| Donors and institutions | The grant fund | **grant fund** |
| Venues and businesses | Paid work with the pay stated | **partner** |

What we never say, to anyone, is one list: [claims.md, "Never say"](claims.md#never-say). Each section below adds only what is specific to that audience.

**Faith framing.** Use **the garden** claim as written. Creative-facing copy names The Garden plainly. Copy for civic funders, foundations, and public partners describes the platform and lists The Garden as one community on it. Both are true.

## 1 · Creatives

**Who.** Working and side-hustle creatives in San Diego County first. Density matters more than reach. Church worship and media teams, art walks and open mics in North County and downtown, film and writing groups, juried shows on the county arts calendar, design and photo people already freelancing. National portfolio sites come later.

**What they want,** in order: to get paid for work they already do, to stop chasing invoices and goodwill, and to be around people who take the craft and the faith seriously.

**Lead with** paid work posted by churches, businesses, and nonprofits. Then **backing**. Then **pool**. Close with **join**.

**Email**

Subject: **Your work deserves to be paid for**

> Hi {name}. I saw {specific piece} and wanted to reach out.
>
> Most creative work in San Diego gets found in a Facebook group and paid for out of someone's goodwill. I'm building creatives.exchange to change that.
>
> Three things about it:
>
> 1. Churches, businesses, and nonprofits post paid work, and each post says what it pays.
> 2. People can back you. When someone backs you for $100, you get $90. The other $10 runs the platform.
> 3. Joining is free. Membership is $10 a month when you want to apply to paid work or ask the grant fund to back a project.
>
> The Garden is the Christian creative community on it. That is where this started, and probably why this email is coming from me.
>
> On November 6 we put funded work on stage at Lightchurch in Encinitas.
>
> Could I have 20 minutes this week? I'd rather hear what you're making than pitch you.
>
> Rick

**Text.** "Hey {name}, loved {piece}. I'm building creatives.exchange: paid work for creatives, and people who back them. Free to join. Got 20 minutes before our Nov 6 showcase?"

**If they ask what the $10 gets them:** use **membership**, word for word.

**Don't say**
- Don't frame the free account as a trial, or membership as the "real" account.
- Don't lead with grants. Lead with paid work. A grant is a maybe; a gig is a date and a number.

## 2 · Hosts and community leaders

**Who.** People already running paid creative gatherings: workshop and cohort teachers on Eventbrite, leaders of dues-charging groups on Meetup, community owners on Skool, hackathon and game-jam organizers.

**What they want.** Fewer tools, and to be paid for the room they already run.

**Lead with** **host split**. Then the tools: rosters, sign-ups, sessions, events, and payments in one place.

**Email**

Subject: **You're running this out of Venmo and a spreadsheet**

> Hi {name}. I've been following {group}.
>
> creatives.exchange lets you bring your group, keep your name and your way of doing things, and sell classes, cohorts, and memberships. Hosting is free. You keep 90% of what you sell.
>
> If you also give out money, such as contest prizes or a grant at the end of a cohort, there is a $50 a month plan that handles collecting it and paying it out, so you can stop doing that by hand.
>
> Could I get 20 minutes to walk you through it? I'd also love to have {group} at our November 6 showcase in Encinitas.
>
> Rick

**Text.** "Hey {name}, are you running {group}'s sign-ups and payments out of a few different tools? I built one place for that. Hosting is free and you keep 90% of what you sell. 20 minutes this week? Rick, creatives.exchange"

**Don't say**
- Never mention per-member host shares or dues splits. The host money story is **host split**, full stop.

## 3 · Patrons

**Who.** People who already fund creative work by name: backers on Kickstarter and Indiegogo, Patreon supporters of local creatives, the friends and family around every creative we sign, the open-mic and gallery-night regulars who buy the work.

**What they want.** To help one person they believe in, and to see the thing get made.

**Lead with** the person's name. Then **patron**.

**Email**

Subject: **Back {creative first name}'s next project**

> Hi {name}. You know {creative}'s work, so I'll keep this short.
>
> On creatives.exchange you can back {creative} directly. Cover their membership for $10 a month, or back a project of theirs for any amount from $10 up. 90% goes to them. You get updates as the work comes together, and you can be named on it or stay anonymous.
>
> On November 6 we put funded work on stage at Lightchurch in Encinitas. I'd love to have you in the room, or on the free livestream.
>
> Can I send you an invite?
>
> Rick

**Text.** "Hey {name}, you know {creative}'s work. You can back their next project on creatives.exchange. 90% goes to them. Nov 6 showcase in Encinitas. Want an invite?"

**Don't say**
- Always name the person. Never pitch the cause in the abstract.

## 4 · Churches and sponsoring organizations

**Who.** Churches with active worship, media, and arts teams. Creative-arts and worship-director listings, denominational directories, churches already hosting open mics and gallery nights.

**What they want.** To do something concrete for the creatives already in their building.

**Lead with** **coverage**. The church buys memberships on one card and hands out one code.

**Email**

Subject: **Cover the creatives in your church**

> Hi {name}. I'm building creatives.exchange, where creatives find paid work and get backed, and I think {church} would recognize the people on it.
>
> Here is the offer. $10 a month covers one creative's membership. Your church pays for as many as you like on one card, and your creatives claim them with a link or a QR code. A covered membership is a full membership. It changes who pays, never what they can do.
>
> Larger bundles, around $500 a month, are a conversation. Could I get 20 minutes with you this month?
>
> Rick

**Text.** "Hi {name}. creatives.exchange lets {church} cover memberships for its creatives: $10 each, one card, one code. Full memberships, not a discount. 20 minutes to walk you through it? Rick"

**Don't say**
- Never call coverage a donation or imply it is tax-deductible. That is the grant fund, a different door.
- Never call a covered membership a discount, a scholarship, or a lesser tier.

## 5 · Donors and institutions

**Who.** People and institutions that already grant to creative work: arts-council donor lists, community-foundation fund officers, family foundations with arts or faith-and-culture programs, major-donor circles at churches and arts nonprofits.

**What they want.** A sound place for the money, and to see what it produces.

**Lead with** **grant fund**. For institutions, partnerships start at $10,000 a year, by invitation, and only in person.

**Email**

Subject: **The Creative Economy We All Need — November 6, Encinitas**

> Hi {name}. I'd like to invite you to something specific.
>
> On November 6 we are hosting The Creative Economy We All Need at Lightchurch in Encinitas. It is an evening of funded creative work and a ticketed benefit for our grant fund. The fund is run by Abiding Practice, a 501(c)(3), so gifts to it are tax-deductible. About 87% of each gift is granted.
>
> For institutions considering something larger, we run a small number of partnerships by invitation. The evening is the right place to see what the money produces.
>
> May I hold you a seat, or set up 20 minutes before then?
>
> Rick

**Text.** "Hi {name}. Nov 6 at Lightchurch in Encinitas: an evening of funded creative work, benefiting our grant fund. Gifts are tax-deductible through a 501(c)(3). I'd like to hold you a seat. 20 minutes first? Rick"

**Don't say**
- The 87% figure belongs to the grant fund alone. Never mix it with the platform's numbers.
- Never promise a donor a say in who gets a grant.
- Never pitch the $10,000 tier cold or in writing.

## 6 · Venues and businesses

**Who.** Businesses that host or hire creative work: restaurants, bars, coffee shops, breweries, and galleries with live music or art nights; coworking and event spaces with slow hours; print shops, studios, and gear houses; local businesses that commission murals, menus, photography, and video. Small independent places first.

**What they want.** A reliable act on Friday night without the hassle, or good work for a fair price.

**Lead with** **partner**. For a venue with a regular night, show the recurring gig: post the night and the pay once, see who is available for each date with clips to listen to, and pick.

**Email**

Subject: **Live music on {night}, without the chasing**

> Hi {name}. {business} keeps coming up when creatives around here talk about where things happen.
>
> creatives.exchange connects local creatives with places like yours. Post the night and what it pays, once. Creatives mark the dates they can play and attach clips. You listen, pick who plays each date, and pay them the way you already do.
>
> You can also post one-off paid work, such as a mural, a menu, or photos, or offer a room on a slow night.
>
> Could I come by for 20 minutes and show you who is making things nearby?
>
> Rick

**Text.** "Hi {name}. creatives.exchange connects local creatives with places like {business}. Post a night and what it pays, see who's available with clips, and pick. 20 minutes this week? Rick"

**Don't say**
- Never pitch this as advertising, and never promise a crowd.
- If they want a write-off, that is the grant fund conversation. Don't mix the two in one ask.
- Don't quote a price for venues yet. It is not set.

## Flyers

Flyer files live in `docs/flyers/`. Build a new one from the claims: one headline that states the action, one subhead, three bullets that are each a claim or a fact, and one link. No money sentence that is not in claims.md.

## Sequencing

**Warm network first.** Start with people who already know a creative on the platform: the friends, congregations, and regulars around our first creatives and hosts. Every warm ask names a specific person and a specific project. Cold lists wait until the warm layer has produced backed projects we can point to.

**November 6 is the reason to reach out in September and October.** Creatives get a stage. Hosts get their people in the room. Patrons and churches see the work they helped pay for. Donors and institutions get the room where partnerships are made. Venues get named from it. Book the 20-minute conversations in September. Turn them into seats in the room in October.
