# creatives.exchange — constituent outreach playbook

v0.1 · 2026-08-31 · owner: Rick

Six audiences, one story. Each section: who they are and where to find them, the pitch, an email, a text, flyer copy, and what never to say to them. Everything here is in the locked product voice (product plan §3.1): down-to-earth and real, headlines state the action or fact, numbers persuade, no metaphor riffs, "creative" never "artist."

---

## One story, six doors

The elevator pitch everyone hears first, whatever door they came in:

> **Most creative work gets found in a Facebook group and paid for out of somebody's goodwill. creatives.exchange exists to change that.** It's where creatives find paid work, get backed by people who believe in them, and apply to a real grant fund. Joining is free and stays free. When someone backs your project you keep 100% — our fee is paid by the backer, not taken out of your money. The grant fund is administered by Abiding Practice, a 501(c)(3), and every grant is published. **The Garden** is the Christian creative community inside it, and it's where this started. On **November 6** we're putting funded work on stage at Lightchurch in Encinitas — a ticketed fundraiser for the Grant Fund, with a free livestream.

The same story, angled per door:

| Constituent | Their sentence |
|---|---|
| Creatives | "Get your work funded. You keep 100% — the backer covers our fee, not you." |
| Hosts | "Get paid to gather the people you already gather." |
| Individual patrons | "Back a specific person. 100% reaches them. You're credited on the work." |
| Churches & orgs | "$10 covers a creative's seat. The public grant list shows what it did." |
| Donors & institutions | "Tax-deductible through a 501(c)(3). 90% of every dollar is granted." |
| Venues & businesses | "Offer the space or post the budget. Your name goes on the finished work." |

**Universal don't-say list** — these hold with every audience, in every channel:

- Never mention internal splits, "home community," or per-member host shares. The only money sentence a member ever hears is the receipt: **"$5 funds other creatives' projects · $5 runs the place."**
- **"Donate"** is used for exactly one thing: the General Grant Fund (tax-deductible, administered by Abiding Practice, a 501(c)(3)). Everywhere else the words are **back** and **fund**.
- Describing what the platform is for — **"get your work funded"** — is correct and we should say it plainly. What we never do is promise a *specific* person a specific outcome: that their project will be funded, that they'll win a grant, or a dollar figure they'll earn.
- Never "raffle," "drawing," or any chance-based mechanic. Grant programs are skill contests and judged or voted grants only.
- Never "artist." The word is **creative**.
- **Faith framing, resolved (2026-09):** the platform is open to any creative. **The Garden is the Christian creative community inside it, and it is where this started.** Creative-facing copy names The Garden plainly rather than hiding it. Copy aimed at civic funders, foundations, and public partners describes the platform and lists The Garden as one community on it. Both are true; neither is a dodge.
- Never pitch the seat as the way in. The way in is free, always: "Money is never the only door."

## 1 · Creatives (free → seat)

**Target.** Working and side-hustle creatives in San Diego County first — density matters more than reach. Church worship and media teams, art walks and open mics in North County and downtown, film and writing groups, juried shows on the county arts calendar, design and photo people already freelancing. National portfolio sites (Behance, Dribbble, FilmFreeway, Bandcamp) are a second wave, not the first.

**What they actually want,** in order: to get paid for work they're already doing; to stop chasing invoices and goodwill; to be around people who take both the craft and the faith seriously.

**The pitch.** Most creative work around here gets found in a Facebook group and paid for out of somebody's goodwill. This is built to change that. Find paid work posted by churches, businesses and nonprofits. Get backed by people who believe in your project — and keep 100% of it, because our fee is paid by the backer, not taken out of your money. Apply to a grant fund that is real money, administered by a 501(c)(3), with every grant published. Joining is free and stays free.

**Email**

Subject: **Your work deserves to be paid for**

> Hi {name} — I saw {specific piece} and wanted to reach out.
>
> Most creative work in San Diego gets found in a Facebook group and paid for out of someone's goodwill. I'm building creatives.exchange to change that — a place where creatives find paid work, get backed, and can apply to a real grant fund.
>
> Three things that make it different:
>
> — **You keep 100% of what a backer gives you.** Our fee is paid by them at checkout, not taken out of your money. No other platform does that.
> — **The grant fund is real.** Funded by churches and donors, administered by Abiding Practice — a 501(c)(3) — and every grant is published where anyone can read it.
> — **Joining is free and stays free.** A seat is $10/month only when you want to be funded.
>
> The Garden is the Christian creative community inside it, which is where this started and probably why you're getting this email from me.
>
> On November 6 we're putting funded work on stage at Lightchurch in Encinitas.
>
> Could I have 20 minutes this week? I'd rather hear what you're making than pitch you.
>
> — Rick

**Text/DM.** "Hey {name} — loved {piece}. I'm building creatives.exchange: creatives finding paid work and getting backed, and you keep 100% because the backer covers our fee. Free to join. Got 20 min before our Nov 6 showcase?"

**Flyer**

- Headline: **Get your work funded.**
- Subhead: Paid work, backing, and a real grant fund — for creatives who are tired of exposure. Free to join.
- Bullets:
  - **You keep 100%.** When someone backs your project, our fee is paid by them, not taken out of your money.
  - **A grant fund that's actually funded.** Money from churches and donors, administered by a 501(c)(3), every grant published.
  - **Paid work worth applying to.** Posted by churches, businesses and nonprofits who want to hire creatives.
  - **People who get it.** The Garden — the Christian creative community inside creatives.exchange.
- The seat: Free to join and stay free. $10/month when you want to be funded — start projects, apply to paid work, propose to the grant fund. Half of it funds another creative's project.
- CTA: **creatives.exchange**

**What makes the $10 worth it,** if they ask directly: start your own fundable projects, apply to paid work, propose to the grant fund, join member tables, and put on events. A free account can see everything and belong anywhere; the seat is what makes you fundable.

**Don't say.**
- Don't promise *this person* will get funded or win a grant. "Get your work funded" describes the platform; "your project will get funded" is a promise we can't keep.
- Don't claim a payout timeline we haven't set. "You keep 100%" is the claim. Speed is not.
- Don't lead with the public grant list. Transparency is the *backer's* and *donor's* selling point; to a creative it can read as their income being on display.
- Don't frame the free account as a trial, or the seat as the "real" membership.

## 2 · Hosts and community leaders (free host → $50 Leader tier)

**Target.** People already running paid creative gatherings: workshop and cohort teachers charging on Eventbrite; leaders of dues-charging groups on Meetup (film clubs, writing clubs, critique circles); community owners in Skool's public directory; hackathon and game-jam organizers on Devpost and itch.io.

**The pitch.** Hosting is free, and you keep 90% of anything you sell — classes, cohorts, premium tiers. You're already running a grant program in Venmo and a spreadsheet: collecting money, picking a winner, paying them by hand. The Leader tier ($50/mo) turns that into real rails — funded contests, cohort-ending grants, hackathons — with a public ledger your people can check.

**Email**

Subject: **You're already doing this in Venmo and a spreadsheet**

> Hi {name} — I've been following {group}, and you're running the thing I built rails for.
>
> creatives.exchange makes hosting free: bring your group, keep your name and your way of doing things, and keep 90% of anything you sell — classes, cohorts, premium tiers. No platform rent.
>
> The part I want to show you is the Leader tier: run funded contests, cohort-ending grants, and hackathons with real rails instead of Venmo and a spreadsheet — every dollar in and out on a public ledger your members can check.
>
> Could I get 20 minutes to walk you through it? I'd also love to have {group} at our November 6 showcase in Encinitas.
>
> — Rick

**Text/DM.** "Hey {name} — you're running {group}'s grants out of Venmo and a spreadsheet, right? I built the rails for exactly that. Hosting's free, you keep 90% of what you sell. 20 min this week? — Rick, creatives.exchange"

**Flyer**

- Headline: **Get paid to gather the people you already gather.**
- Subhead: Hosting is free. You keep 90% of anything you sell.
- Bullets:
  - Classes, cohorts, premium tiers — your price, your name, 90% yours.
  - Leader tier: run funded contests and cohort-ending grants on real rails.
  - Every dollar in and out on a public ledger your members can read.
- CTA: creatives.exchange/hosts — talk to us.

**Don't say.** Never "raffle" or any chance-based mechanic — skill contests and judged grants only. Never mention per-member host shares or dues splits; the host money story is "keep 90% of what you sell," full stop.

## 3 · Individual patrons and backers

**Target.** People who already fund creative work by name: active backers on Kickstarter and Indiegogo (visible on campaign backer lists in your network); Patreon supporters of local creatives; the friends-and-family circle around every creative you sign; open-mic and gallery-night regulars who buy the work.

**The pitch.** Back a specific person, not a cause. Cover a creative's seat for $10 a month, or back a project with $10 to $500 — 90% reaches the creative immediately, you're credited on the work, and every dollar shows up on a public ledger you can check any time.

**Email**

Subject: **Back {creative first name}'s next project**

> Hi {name} — you know {creative}'s work, so I'll keep this short.
>
> I'm building creatives.exchange, where backing a creative is specific and visible. You can cover {creative}'s seat for $10/mo, or back a project of theirs with anything from $10 to $500. 90% goes to them, immediately — and every allocation is published on a ledger you can read without an account, so you see exactly what your money did. You're credited on the finished work.
>
> On November 6 we're putting funded work on stage at Lightchurch in Encinitas. I'd love to have you in the room — or on the free livestream.
>
> Can I send you an invite?
>
> — Rick

**Text/DM.** "Hey {name} — you know {creative}'s work. On creatives.exchange you can back their next project directly: 90% reaches them immediately, your name's on the work, every dollar on a public ledger. Nov 6 showcase in Encinitas — want an invite?"

**Flyer**

- Headline: **Back a creative. See every dollar.**
- Subhead: Cover a seat for $10/mo, or back a project from $10 to $500.
- Bullets:
  - 90% goes to the creative, immediately.
  - Every allocation on a public ledger — check it any time, no account.
  - You're credited on the work you helped make happen.
- CTA: creatives.exchange/back — pick a creative.

**Don't say.** Never "donate" — backing a creative is not a donation and is not tax-deductible; the words are back and fund. Never pitch the abstract cause — always name the person.

## 4 · Churches and sponsoring organizations

**Target.** Churches with active worship, media, and arts teams: creative-arts and worship-director listings on ChurchStaffing; denominational and network directories (your denomination's district list, church-planting networks); churches already running arts ministries or hosting open mics and gallery nights in your county.

**The pitch.** Coverage codes let your church put creatives on the platform at $10 a seat, on one subscription — and a covered seat is a full seat, identical to paying your own way. Your church is credited on every covered creative's story updates, and the public allocations ledger means your congregation can see exactly what the sponsorship did.

A covered seat is a full seat — sponsorship changes who pays, never what they can do. {Church} is credited on every covered creative's story updates, and every allocation is published on a ledger anyone can read. You can show your congregation exactly what the money did.

>
> — Rick

**Text/DM.** "Hi {name} — creatives.exchange lets {church} cover creatives' seats at $10/seat, one subscription. Full seats, church credited on their work, every dollar on a public ledger your congregation can see. 20 min to walk you through it? — Rick"

**Flyer**

- Subhead: Coverage codes: $10 a seat, one subscription, claimed by link or QR.

## 5 · Donors and institutional big backers

**Target.** People and institutions that already grant to creative work: state and city arts-council grantee and donor lists (published annually); community-foundation donor-advised fund officers; family foundations with arts or faith-and-culture programs in their 990s; major-donor circles at churches and arts nonprofits in your network.

**The pitch.** The General Grant Fund is tax-deductible, administered by Abiding Practice, a 501(c)(3) — about 87 cents of every dollar is granted, and every grant is published on a public ledger. For institutions, partnerships start at $10,000 a year, by invitation. November 6 is where those conversations happen in person.

**Email**

Subject: **The Creative Economy We All Need — November 6, Encinitas**

> Hi {name} — I'd like to invite you to something specific.
>
> On November 6 we're hosting **The Creative Economy We All Need** at Lightchurch in Encinitas — an evening about the engine that funds creative work, and a ticketed benefit for our General Grant Fund. The fund is tax-deductible, administered by Abiding Practice, a 501(c)(3); roughly 87% of every dollar is granted to creatives, and every grant is published on a ledger anyone can read.
>
> For institutions considering something larger, we run a small number of partnerships starting at $10,000/year, by invitation. The evening is the right place to see what the money actually produces.
>
> May I hold you a seat — or set up 20 minutes before then?
>
> — Rick

**Text/DM.** "Hi {name} — Nov 6, Lightchurch in Encinitas: an evening of funded creative work, benefiting our Grant Fund (tax-deductible via a 501(c)(3), ~87% granted, all of it on a public ledger). I'd like to hold you a seat. 20 min first? — Rick"

## 6 · Venue and business partners

**Target.** Businesses that host or hire creative work: coffee shops, breweries, and galleries already listing open mics and art nights on Eventbrite; coworking and event spaces with off-peak hours; print shops, studios, and gear houses with spare capacity; local businesses that commission murals, menus, photography, and video (your chamber of commerce directory is the list).

**The pitch.** Offer your space or services, or post a paid project with a real budget — a commission is a paid project. Either way your business is credited on the finished work, and the story of that work carries your name to exactly the local audience you want.

**Email**

Subject: **Your space, their work, your name on it**

> Hi {name} — {business} keeps coming up when creatives around here talk about where things happen.
>
> creatives.exchange is where creative work gets funded in the open. Two ways a business plugs in: offer what you have — a room on slow nights, print capacity, gear — or post a paid project with a declared budget (a mural, a campaign, an event's visuals) and creatives apply. Either way, {business} is credited on the finished work, and the work's story carries your name.
>
> If visibility is the goal, sponsorship tiers start at $100/mo. But start simpler: could I come by for 20 minutes and show you what's being made nearby?
>
> — Rick

**Text/DM.** "Hi {name} — creatives.exchange connects local creatives with businesses like {business}. Offer space or services, or post a paid gig with a real budget — your name goes on the finished work. 20 min this week to show you what's being made nearby? — Rick"

**Flyer**

- Headline: **Put your business's name on the work.**
- Subhead: Offer space or services, or post paid projects with a real budget.
- Bullets:
  - A commission is a paid project — declare the budget, creatives apply.
  - Your business is credited on the finished work and its story page.
  - Sponsorship tiers from $100/mo when visibility is the goal.
- CTA: creatives.exchange/partners — list your first offer.

**Don't say.** Never pitch this as advertising or promise reach numbers — the credit is on real work, not an ad slot. If they want a write-off, that's the Grant Fund conversation, not a sponsorship tier — don't mix the two in one ask.

## Sequencing

**Warm network first.** September opens with people who already know a creative on the platform — the friends, congregations, and regulars around our first creatives and hosts. Every warm ask names a specific person and a specific project. Cold lists wait until the warm layer has produced backed projects the ledger can show.

**November 6 is the outreach excuse for September–October.** Every constituent gets a reason to say yes before they're asked to spend: creatives get a stage, hosts get their people in the room, patrons and churches get to see what their money did, donors and institutions get the room where partnerships get landed, and venue partners get named from it. Book the 20-minute conversations in September; convert them to seats in the room in October; let the evening do the closing.
